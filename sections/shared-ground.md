# Shared Ground

This section states what binds us before we debate how to build. It distills Kudora’s vision into everyday attitudes that make collaboration easier than conflict and turn differences into compounding strength.

## What we share (the purpose)

- **Recognition-first economy.** We route power and rewards to people who create real value—builders, educators, organizers, reviewers—not just capital. Kudos (recognition) sits at the center.
- **Culture before machinery.** Kudora is a place to build a humane digital society, not just another chain. We measure our health by how well people can work together with dignity and momentum.
- **Frameworks for reuse.** We prefer reusable building blocks over one-off features so that individual wins become shared acceleration.

## Non-negotiables (our load-bearing values)

- **Ethics as infrastructure.** Guardrails are encoded from day one so that “doing the right thing” is also the easiest, most rewarding path.
- **Recognition over wealth.** Governance influence is earned through contribution, not just bought with tokens.
- **Transparency over opacity.** Decisions, reputations, and flows of value should be auditable end-to-end.
- **Inclusion over gatekeeping.** Many kinds of work create value (code, design, education, governance, ops), and all are welcome and visible.
- **Non-forkable culture.** Code can fork; trust should not. We protect shared norms so our culture travels with us as we scale.

## Differences → synergy (how we fit, not split)

We expect disagreements on licenses, roadmaps, taste, and tactics. We treat them as raw material for synergy:

- **Shared aim, varied paths.** Keep the destination fixed (recognition-first, ethics-guided) while encouraging multiple implementations that can be compared, combined, or retired based on evidence.
- **Synergy chain.** Every good solution becomes a framework others can adopt—so each new project launches faster using more of what already works.
- **Evidence over ego.** The Recognition Loop and downstream reuse tell us what actually works; we reward what compounds, not what is loudest.

## Default mindsets (attitudes before tools)

- **Assume good intent; insist on clarity.** Steelman others, ask for specifics, and leave decision records that explain the “why,” not only the “what.”
- **Time-box parallelism.** When disagreement persists, run small, time-boxed alternatives and compare with shared metrics (reuse, adoption, coordination latency).
- **Make reuse the default.** Start from an existing framework before inventing a new one; attribute upstream and document improvements for the next team.
- **Welcome the soft work.** Onboarding, conflict mediation, and documentation earn real recognition—no contribution is invisible.

## Collaboration commitments (what I owe the team)

- **Context first.** I bring problem statements, constraints, and success criteria—not just opinions.
- **Cite and attribute.** I name prior work and people; reuse is a feature, not a flaw.
- **Discuss in the open.** I keep deliberation and trade-offs visible so others can learn and audit.
- **Disagree and commit—then measure.** Once a decision is made, I execute wholeheartedly and evaluate with shared metrics.
- **Escalate respectfully.** If stuck, I invite steward facilitation rather than entrench.

## Anti-patterns we avoid

- **Hype over substance.** Pumping narratives without shipping value corrodes trust; we measure health, not theatrics.
- **Extraction over contribution.** Systems that pay spectators more than creators hollow out communities; we invert that.
- **Opaque decision-making.** If people can’t see how choices are made, they won’t trust or adopt them.
- **Forks as first resort.** Forks are legitimate but costly; we try adaptation, attribution, and stewardship before splitting.

## How recognition binds disagreement

The Recognition Loop turns collaboration into a positive-sum game: value → verification → Kudos → reputation → influence → better matches → bigger impact → repeat. It rewards the work that *others* build on, making generous builders the most influential—and making consensus easier to reach over time.

We pair this with public metrics (Recognition Velocity, Synergy Index, Coordination Latency, etc.) so outcomes—not personalities—decide which approaches scale.

## The role of EthicDAO (culture’s immune system)

EthicDAO keeps our values non-forkable: it guides principles, detects harmful patterns early, and provides aligned paths for escalation and resolution. It does not replace community agency; it protects it.

## Language we share

Clear words prevent confused work. When in doubt, align on the Glossary (Kudos, Recognition Loop, Framework Layer, Synergy Chain, Trust Integrity, etc.) before debating solutions.
