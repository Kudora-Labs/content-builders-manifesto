# Social Norms

These are the everyday behaviors that make collaboration easier than conflict. They are lightweight on process and heavy on mindset. If we all keep to these, differences turn into compounding strength.

## Our defaults

- **Kind, clear, direct.** Say what you mean, with care. Avoid loaded language and sarcasm.
- **Public by default.** Discuss in open threads/issues so others can learn and help. Private DMs are for sensitive topics only.
- **Evidence over volume.** Bring signals (users, data, reuse, constraints) rather than bigger paragraphs.
- **Assume good intent.** Start from respect; critique ideas, not people.
- **Short, scoped steps.** Propose the smallest valuable next move and who owns it.

## How we give feedback

- **Describe, don’t label.** Use *Situation → Behavior → Impact → Request* (SBI-R).  
  *“In yesterday’s review (S), the API shape changed without a note (B), which broke my integration (I). Could you add a migration note next time? (R)”*
- **Balance speed and learning.** If it’s a quick fix, submit a PR; if it’s a bigger shift, open a brief issue first.
- **Praise is specific.** Credit the exact decision, doc, or patch that helped you.

## Reviews & responses

- **Review SLAs.** Aim to respond within 48h on weekdays. If you need more time, leave a quick “seen—back by <date>”.
- **Right-sized PRs.** Prefer small, focused changes with a one-paragraph summary and test notes.
- **Teach as you review.** Suggest patterns or examples rather than “no” without context.
- **Single owner, many helpers.** Each task has a DRI (directly responsible individual). Helpers unblock, not override.

## Decisions in practice

- **Disagree, then commit.** After a decision record (ADR) lands, execute wholeheartedly—even if you preferred another path. If learning contradicts the decision, propose a revisit with evidence.
- **Parallel, time-boxed trials.** When disagreement is about *means*, we allow small, time-boxed alternatives with shared metrics.
- **No hallway vetoes.** Off-thread lobbying after a public decision is an anti-pattern. Reopen the thread with new evidence instead.

## Inclusion & accessibility

- **Multiple kinds of work matter.** Code, docs, design, community, education, governance, operations—each earns recognition.
- **Readable English.** Prefer simple, non-idiomatic English. Explain acronyms on first use. Provide brief summaries for long threads.
- **Time zones are real.** Don’t assume synchronous replies. Recap decisions and record meeting notes for those who couldn’t join.
- **Accessible artifacts.** Use alt text for images, captions for videos, and high-contrast diagrams when possible.

## Attribution & recognition

- **Credit upstream.** Name libraries, ideas, and people you build on. Link them in READMEs/ADRs.
- **Surface invisible work.** Welcome docs, onboarding, conflict mediation, and support. Log them so recognition can find them.
- **Share the win.** When two paths merge, both teams are credited for integration, not just the “winner.”

## Conflict & repair

- **Name the snag early.** If you feel blocked or unheard, say so plainly and propose a concrete next step.
- **Cool-down > escalation.** Use a brief pause if tone slips. Then restart with “what we both want” and constraints.
- **Ask for a steward.** If stuck, invite a steward to facilitate. Document outcomes so we don’t re-fight the same fight.

## AI & authorship

- **Disclose assistance.** If AI helped, state it briefly and verify facts/outputs. You own the result.
- **Keep provenance.** Link prompts or generation steps when it aids review and reproducibility.

## Brand & representation

- **Speak for yourself, cite the project.** Use “I” for personal views; link to the Manifesto for shared positions.
- **No confusion.** Don’t imply endorsement by the project unless there’s an approved decision or announcement.

## Anti-patterns we call out early

- Drive-by “no” without an alternative or constraint.  
- Sarcasm, ad hominem, or dismissing experience/novices.  
- Private lobbying after a public decision.  
- Scope creep without re-agreeing constraints.  
- Withholding information to win a debate.  

When any of these appear, we’ll name it, repair it, and return to the shared goal.

## Newcomer pledge

- We will welcome you, answer “obvious” questions, and point to good first issues.
- We will prefer patterns and docs over one-off answers.
- We will recognize your first contributions publicly.

## Personal commitment

> I will be kind, clear, and specific. I will argue from evidence and constraints. I will disagree in the open, commit after the decision, and share credit generously. I will leave the project’s culture better than I found it.

---
These norms aren’t red tape; they are how trust compounds here. Hold the bar, help each other reach it, and we’ll move faster together than any of us could alone.
