# Stewardship

**Stewards** are culture keepers and friction removers. They don’t “own” outcomes; they **own the path** to fair, fast, value-aligned outcomes. Their first tool is facilitation, not authority.

---

## Purpose (why this role exists)

- **Protect the shared ground.** Keep debates tied to goals, constraints, and evidence.
- **Keep flow.** Unstick stalled work, set timeboxes, and ensure decisions land.
- **Guard fairness.** Surface minority views, manage conflicts of interest, and keep recognition honest.
- **Minimize blast radius.** Catch risks early (security, brand, legal, ethics).

---

## Principles

- **Lightest effective touch.** Nudge → structure → mediate → decide process → escalate.
- **Ends over egos.** Anchor to values, constraints, and agreed metrics.
- **Transparent by default.** Summarize interventions on-thread (except sensitive cases).
- **Document or it didn’t happen.** Outcomes and rationales are logged.

---

## When a Steward Steps In (triggers)

- Review SLAs missed; PRs or RFCs stalled.
- Debate loops or “hallway vetoes.”
- Scope creep without re-agreeing constraints.
- Conflicting decisions across teams.
- Safety, security, brand, or licensing risk.
- Repeated norms violations or interpersonal friction.

---

## Responsibilities

**Facilitation**
- Reframe to a clear problem statement and constraints.
- Choose a decision mode (lazy consensus, parallel trials, consent/objection handling, council ruling).
- Set **timeboxes**, checkpoints, and owners.

**Quality & Safety**
- Ensure risk-tiered changes have the right lane reviewers.
- Require kill-switch/rollback in risky ADRs.
- Route sensitive disclosures via private channels.

**Fairness & Integrity**
- Watch for conflicts of interest; request recusals when needed.
- Keep minority/novice views visible and recorded.
- Recommend recognition adjustments in edge cases; publish rationale.

**Exceptions & Escalation**
- Vet **Commons Exceptions** (scope, reason, duration, exit criteria).
- Recommend; **Council/EthicDAO** approves wide-impact exceptions.
- Escalate Ends-level conflicts or deadlocks that exceed the blast radius.

---

## Limits

- Stewards do not override technical reviewers on quality within their lane.
- Stewards do not set “the answer” for Ends; they escalate to Council/EthicDAO.
- Private mediation is for de-escalation, not decision-making; decisions are logged publicly.

---

## Selection, Term, Rotation

- **Appointment:** Proposed via RFC or nomination; evidence of facilitation skill and trust.
- **Term:** 6–12 months, renewable.
- **Rotation:** Encourage overlap to avoid single-point-of-failure and burnout.
- **Removal/timeout:** For repeated breaches (bias, missed SLAs, opacity); requires a short public rationale and a path to return.

---

## Steward SLAs

- Acknowledge ping within **24h** on weekdays (“Seen; next step by <date>”).
- Propose a mode/timebox within **48–72h** for stuck items.
- Close or escalate within the agreed timebox.
- Post a one-paragraph **Outcome Note** after mediation/decision.

---

## Intervention Ladder (use the lightest step that works)

1. **Nudge** — ask for problem statement, constraints, or one-pager.
2. **Structure** — set decision mode, timebox, and metrics.
3. **Parallelism** — run small, fair experiments with shared inputs.
4. **Mediation** — facilitate a short, structured conversation; capture agreements.
5. **Process decision** — pick the mode/owner/guardrails when the group can’t.
6. **Escalation** — hand to Council/EthicDAO with a neutral summary.

---

## Mediation Flow (30–45 minutes)

1. **Start with shared aim & constraints.** One minute per person to restate.
2. **Steelman round.** Each side states the other’s best case.
3. **Options table.** List 2–3 credible paths; note risks/mitigations.
4. **Mode & metrics.** Decide on experiment vs. immediate call; define success measures.
5. **Owners & dates.** Name DRI, reviewers, checkpoints, and kill-switch.
6. **Outcome note.** Post summary and next steps to the original thread.

---

## Recognition & Conflicts

- **Recognition:** Stewards earn Kudos for unblocking, clarity, and integrity (not for “picking winners”). Teaching and de-escalation apply multipliers.
- **Conflicts of Interest:** Disclose ties; recuse from recognition attestations or decision mode selection when conflicted. Another steward steps in.

---

## Metrics (dashboards to keep us honest)

- **Dispute cycle time** — first ping → outcome note.
- **Review SLA hit rate** — % reviews on time.
- **Reopen rate** — % ADRs reopened due to poor process.
- **Recognition reversals** — upheld vs. reversed after appeals.
- **Culture signals** — flagged anti-patterns resolved, onboarding satisfaction.

Targets and current values appear in the public governance dashboard.

---

## Anti-Patterns (and steward responses)

- **Perma-threading.** *Response:* one-pager + timebox; or move to experiment.
- **Goalpost moving.** *Response:* freeze criteria; assess on agreed metrics.
- **Private lobbying.** *Response:* ask to summarize in public; ignore off-thread pressure.
- **Decider drift.** *Response:* re-affirm DRI/scope; escalate only if Ends/Constraints.
- **Hero bottleneck.** *Response:* assign a co-reviewer; split work; rotate owners.

## Becoming a Steward (readiness checklist)

- Demonstrated facilitation in 3+ threads or RFCs.
- Consistent review timeliness and clarity.
- Evidence of unbiased behavior across lanes.
- Familiarity with ADRs, RFCs, recognition, licensing defaults, and brand rules.
- Willingness to document outcomes and accept feedback.

Nominate via RFC with links to evidence and a proposed term/scope.

---

## Steward Oath (personal commitment)

> I will use the lightest intervention that restores flow. I will anchor debates to goals, constraints, and evidence. I will make process transparent, protect minority views, and escalate only when necessary. I will leave clear notes so others can learn without me.

---

**Bottom line:** Stewards keep momentum and fairness intact. They make it easier to do the right thing—and harder to get stuck.
